# LLM Evaluation Pass

The LLM pass is the **primary** evaluator of job postings. It replaces the
regex/keyword scoring (which now serves only as a deterministic *pre-filter* to
surface the top ~25 candidates whose full descriptions get fetched). The LLM
reads the actual job descriptions and produces a **resume ↔ posting fit score**
that is more accurate than keyword matching.

## What the score is (and is NOT)

`llm_score` is a **comparison of the candidate's actual experience (across ALL
uploaded resumes) to the job posting** — an evaluation of potential fit for the
role based on what he has demonstrably done. Specifically:

**The score IS based on:**
- Do the role's responsibilities map onto his demonstrated experience?
  (enterprise applications portfolio, SaaS/cloud, AI governance, data platform,
  vendor/RFP/budget, ITIL/service management, collaboration tools, people
  leadership, technical-architecture background)
- Does the seniority (Manager / Director) match his level? (VP/C-level is excluded — see gating)
- Is it a role type he would actually take? (exclusions below are fit mismatches)

**The score is NOT based on (record as notes, do NOT move the number):**
- **Location** — informational, EXCEPT: a non-GTA role that ALSO requires in-office days must be flagged AND capped below 8.0 (max 7.9).
- **Language** (French/bilingual) — informational only
- **On-site vs. hybrid vs. remote policy** — informational only
- **Company brand / sector prestige** — irrelevant to fit

## Inputs (all under /opt/data/resumes/)

- `CANDIDATE_PROFILE.md` — the aggregate of ALL uploaded resumes (the union of
  all four). Grounding for the evaluator.
- `deep_results.json` — the top-N fetched postings, including a `description`
  field (full job-description text). Regenerated by `scripts/deep_dive.py`.

## Output

- `llm_results.json` — one object per evaluated role (schema below). Consumed by
  `scripts/build_scored.py` to render the primary "LLM Evaluation" section.

## Schema (llm_results.json — array of objects)

```json
{
  "title": "Director, Enterprise Technology & Products",
  "company": "Brock University",
  "link": "https://...",
  "location": "St. Catharines, Ontario, Canada",
  "source": "LinkedIn",
  "llm_score": 8.7,
  "verdict": "Strong",
  "recommendation": "Apply",
  "fit_summary": "One or two sentences on the experience fit (or gap).",
  "score_rationale": "1-3 sentences explaining the numeric llm_score: what experience raised it, what gap capped it.",
  "strengths": ["enterprise apps portfolio", "AI governance"],
  "concerns": ["French bilingual required", "Quebec location"],
  "hands_on_dev": false,
  "seniority": "director"
}
```

Field notes:
- `llm_score`: 0–10, one decimal. **Pure resume↔posting experience fit.**
- `verdict`: `Strong` (>=8.0) | `Good` (6.5–7.9) | `Moderate` (5.0–6.4) | `Weak` (<5.0).
- `recommendation`: `Apply` | `Review` | `Skip` — a fit judgment, not a formula.
- `fit_summary`: one or two sentences on the *experience* fit or gap.
- `score_rationale`: 1–3 sentences explaining the numeric `llm_score` — the
  concrete experience that raised it and the gaps that capped/lowered it.
- `strengths` / `concerns`: `concerns` may include practical notes (location,
  language, hybrid policy) but those must NOT have moved `llm_score`.
- `seniority`: `manager` | `director` (use `vp`/`chief` only to mark an EXCLUDED C-level role).
- `hands_on_dev`: true if the role's *primary* job is hands-on coding / dev-team delivery.

## Rubric (how to assign `llm_score`)

Judge **only** the experience fit against `CANDIDATE_PROFILE.md`:

1. **Role scope vs. exclusions (gating).** If the role's primary job is hands-on
   dev-team delivery ("Software Development Manager", "Engineering Manager" leading
   developers in sprints, daily coding) or it's an individual-contributor role with
   no reports/platform ownership, cap the score low (<= 5) — this is the candidate's
   single hardest rule. If the role includes *some* engineering oversight but is
   primarily portfolio/strategy leadership, treat that as a minor concern, not a cap.
2. **Domain overlap.** Enterprise apps, SaaS/cloud, AI/automation/governance,
   data platform, vendor/procurement, service management → strong fit.
   Narrow specialist (pure security ops, pure ERP config, help desk) → weaker.
   Note his historical hands-on engineering (PHP/Sitecore/Solr/Symfony/AWS) — this
   means he can credibly lead technical teams even when he is not coding daily.
3. **Seniority match.** Manager/Director only. Vice President / C-level roles are EXCLUDED — mark `recommendation: "Skip"` and flag in `concerns`, regardless of fit.
4. **Demonstrated-depth gaps.** Flag requirements for deep expertise he has not
   shown (e.g. PhD-level AI research, financial-services model-risk/OSFI compliance,
   manufacturing/SAP-MES specialization) — these are genuine experience gaps.

**Do NOT dock points for:** language or company sector — put those in `concerns` only.

**Two hard rules override the fit score:**
- **Vice President / C-level roles → EXCLUDED** (`recommendation: "Skip"`, flag in concerns).
- **Non-GTA + in-office-days requirement → flag + cap `llm_score` below 8.0** (max 7.9).

## How the LLM pass is run

- **Now / manually:** the agent reads `CANDIDATE_PROFILE.md` + `deep_results.json`,
  evaluates each fetched posting, and writes `llm_results.json`.
- **Daily (cron):** the scheduled job runs in **agent mode** (not `no_agent=True`),
  so the LLM is available: scrape → fetch descriptions (`deep_dive.py`) → LLM
  evaluation → build (`build_scored.py`) → commit/push. See the cron prompt.

## Rendering

`build_scored.py` renders `llm_results.json` as the top "LLM Evaluation — Primary"
section (manager roles first, then directors, each sorted by `llm_score` desc).
The deterministic deep-dive and title-score sections remain below as a secondary
reference, clearly labeled.
